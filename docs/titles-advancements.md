# Advancement titles

The default set of advancement titles, shipped with the mod as `advancements.json`: at the first start it is written
to `config/player_titles/titles/`, where a server can edit it, replace it or remove it (a removed set stays removed).
This document is where the set is made: `scripts/titles/advancement_titles.py` turns its tables into the file.

Every advancement of Minecraft 26.3 that is shown in the game (126, with the five tabs' first ones) gets its own
title, which can't be got any other way, and a "first on the server" title for the first player to get it. The
advancement's name stays the game's; the title is a new word for what the player did, and its description says what
that was.

The advancements are given by their ids only: their names are the game's (its language files, Mojang's texts), not
ours to publish, as in Stats WebUI Plus, which reads them from the game. `scripts/titles/advancement_titles.py
--names` shows them next to the titles, from a local game JAR.

Names of game things in the descriptions are the game's own (`en_us`, `de_de`), written as Stats WebUI Plus writes them:
"hostile mob" / "feindliche Kreatur", adjectives of names in lower case within a German sentence ("einen verzierten
Krug"). The game has no names for structures, so "Endsiedlung" (End City) is the Minecraft Wiki's.

## German forms

Each German title has three forms, and each player chooses theirs (`/titles form`, the name tag in `/titles`; the
server's `defaultForm` for those who haven't): male (the game's own way, "Dorfheld"), female (mostly -in) and neutral
(-ende or -e, "Drachentötende", "Monsterkundige"). Titles that are adjectives or words for anyone
("Eisern", "Todlos", "Bücherwurm") are the same in all three. English titles have one form.

## How they look

- **Colours per tab:** Minecraft teal, Nether red, The End purple, Adventure gold, Husbandry green.
- **By the advancement's frame:** task (91): common, the tab's colour. Goal (10): rare, the tab's gradient. Challenge
  (25): epic, the gradient with an icon at both ends (the item or block it is about). The five hardest challenges:
  legendary, also animated (wave).
- Icons are textures of the game (26.3), all checked.
- No bold, no runes or magic letters here: those are left for the rarest titles (merchant, loot).

## First on the server

- The text gets a word that changes with the rarity, taking turns within it; the cooler ones for the rarer titles:
  - tasks: First, Original, … Prime, Elder, OG, Pro, Top, Chief (Erster, Ur-, Erz-, Altehrwürdiger, OG-, Profi-, Top-,
    Chef-)
  - goals: Veteran, Day One, Elite, Alpha (Altgedienter, Day-One-, Elite-, Alpha-)
  - challenges: Renowned, Supreme, Ultra, Genesis (Berühmter, Ober-, Ultra-, Genesis-)
  - the five hardest: … of Legend, GOAT, … No. 1 (Legendärer, GOAT-, … Nr. 1)
- German titles the same in all three forms ("Eisern", "Neuling") take no adjective before them: Ur- (tasks), Elite-
  (goals), Ultra- (challenges), … Nr. 1 (the five hardest). English titles that are adjectives ("Deathless",
  "Skyborne") get a word after them: … Prime, … Supreme, … of Legend.
- The look: gold with the nether star at both ends, pulsing; with the icon of the title next to the star when it has one ("★⚔ … ⚔★"), and its animation (wave) instead of the pulse. Description: "The first on this server: "
  and the advancement title's description.
- A player who got an advancement first has both titles.

## Notes

- The neutral German forms are the least natural ones ("Wachsende", "Ziegenbootfahrende"); players who don't like
  one choose another form.
- The tabs' first advancements (Newcomer, Nether Guest, Voidgazer, Adventurer, Gatherer) come almost by themselves,
  and have titles too.
- Free the End is the game's end (its credits): epic, though the game makes it a task.
